The Problem

AI can generate video.
It still can't make a film.

01 · Continuity — the core failure

Clip tools are prompt roulette

Today's generators produce impressive 5–10 second one-shots — but the same character comes back as a different person in every take. Locations drift, lighting resets. Nothing cuts together.

02 · Cost

Traditional production is out of reach

Crews, locations, gear, and weeks of post put narrative video beyond nearly every creator, agency, and small studio.

03 · Fragmentation

Five apps per finished minute

Script here, character art there, generation in a third tool, upscaling in a fourth, editing in a fifth. Every hand-off loses fidelity and hours.

The gap between a cool clip and a watchable film is continuity and workflow — not model quality. The models crossed the threshold. Nobody owns the production layer that turns their output into finished stories.

Why Now

The models arrived. The studio layer didn't.

Quality — crossed the threshold

Video generation got good enough

The newest generation of models (Sora 2, Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.0, Wan 2.7) produces footage audiences will actually watch. The bottleneck moved from pixels to production.

Access — commoditized

Every frontier model is an API call

Aggregated APIs put the best models inside any application on day one — and falling per-second costs keep expanding what a $15/month subscriber can render.

Demand — exploding

Creators want narrative, not clips

Short-form drama, AI films, faceless channels, and agency spec work are surging — made by exactly the users clip-generators strand at the editing stage.

The window: whoever owns the workflow becomes the default studio. Model labs compete on generation quality and rent their models to everyone. The durable position is the layer where creators organize scripts, lock their cast, and finish films — that's where projects, assets, and switching costs live.
